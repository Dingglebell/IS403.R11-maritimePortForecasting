# Feature Engineering and Baselines

For each port and forecast origin t, we construct three predictors: estimated cargo over t−6 through t, port calls over the same seven days, and mean daily cargo over t−27 through t. Daily cargo is the sum of the published import and export estimates, in tonnes. The response is total original estimated cargo over t+1 through t+7. Each window is computed within a port on the daily calendar. Observed zeros are retained, while windows with unknown required measurements are excluded. A common 28-day history requirement keeps the evaluation rows identical across all baseline models.

The Task 1 handoff proposes retaining ports with complete cargo and port-call observations throughout the 2019–2024 training period. Eligibility and activity bands use training data only; the alternative 20% positive-day indicator remains available for sensitivity analysis. Final agreement on the shared cohort is pending and is not implied by implementing this pipeline.

We fit pooled models across the selected ports. Seasonal naïve predicts the previous seven-day cargo total. Univariate linear regression uses that total with an intercept. Multivariate linear regression uses all three predictors. Degree-2 regression uses the seven-day cargo total and its square with an intercept. Regressions are estimated by ordinary least squares. Centring and scaling are fitted on training observations only; the polynomial basis is constructed from training-standardized cargo to improve numerical stability.

Observation periods follow the shared configuration: training in 2019–2024, validation in 2025, and a reserved test period in 2026. Training origins end on 24 December 2024, ensuring that their seven-day labels end by 31 December. Validation origins begin on 31 December 2024 and end on 24 December 2025, with labels entirely within 2025. Boundary-crossing labels and incomplete histories are excluded. The Task 2 runner discards observations after the validation boundary before computing features, targets or port eligibility, and it never scores test data.

All four models forecast the same port-origin validation rows. We report MAE and RMSE in tonnes and WAPE as total absolute error divided by total actual cargo, multiplied by 100. WAPE is undefined when actual cargo totals zero. Raw and nonnegative forecast variants are exported separately for joint validation review; the final common clipping rule remains pending. Models are fitted once on training data and are not refitted within validation. Later origins can use newly observed history available by their forecast time.

The analysis assumes that observations through t are available when a forecast is issued. Historical publication times and release vintages are unavailable, so this is a retrospective scenario rather than a verified real-time backtest. Seven-day targets from daily origins overlap and must not be treated as independent weekly volumes. Final model comparisons and operational conclusions require the later frozen test evaluation.
